# Bio and Profile Optimization

Writes the short, high-visibility text that represents the user everywhere on a platform, a LinkedIn headline and About section, an Instagram or X bio, or an equivalent field on a custom platform. This is small in scope but high-value, it's often the first thing anyone reads before deciding whether to follow or read further, and it should sound like the same confirmed voice as everything else this system produces.

## Before writing

Confirm `creator-context` is active for persona, philosophy, and profile, this skill draws directly on the identity statement and core philosophy already established, rather than reinventing a self-description from scratch.

### Draft-or-generate checkpoint

Ask whether the user has an existing bio they want refined, or wants one written from scratch. If they have one, work from what's there, tighten it, fix anything that undersells them or drifts from their confirmed voice, rather than discarding their existing wording entirely.

## Platform-specific structure

**LinkedIn**:
- **Headline** (appears everywhere their name does, strict character limit, roughly 220 characters): should state what they do and who they help or what they're known for, not just a job title. A generic title undersells someone with a clear point of view.
- **About section** (much longer, no strict practical limit but skimmed quickly): can hold a short version of the identity statement and philosophy from `creator-context`, plus concrete credibility markers, then a line inviting connection or stating what kind of content they post.

**Instagram**:
- Very short (roughly 150 characters), often includes line breaks, may include a link. Needs to communicate identity and value fast, sometimes with a personality touch (an emoji, a distinctive phrase) if that fits the confirmed tone.

**X**:
- Short (roughly 160 characters), reads more like a punchy one-liner than a formal bio. Room for a sharper, more opinionated version of the identity than LinkedIn's more formal headline allows.

**Custom platforms**: ask about the field's character limit and structural convention if not already known, don't assume it matches one of the platforms above.

## Writing the bio

Pull directly from the confirmed **Identity statement** and **Core Philosophy** in `creator-context`, don't write a generic professional bio disconnected from the persona work already done. The bio should sound like a compressed version of the same voice the rest of this system produces, not a separate, more formal register.

Avoid generic filler common to bios (words like "passionate," "enthusiast," vague titles like "creative professional") unless the user's own confirmed tone specifically calls for that register. Be specific: what they actually do, for whom, and what makes them worth following, grounded in their real credibility markers from the profile, not invented ones.

Apply the confirmed banlist and punctuation rule here too, a bio is still content, house style applies.

## Presenting the result

Present each platform's bio clearly labeled and ready to paste, with the character count noted if the platform has a strict limit, so the user can confirm it fits before using it. If multiple platforms are being written at once, keep them consistent with each other, same core identity, adjusted for each platform's length and register, not contradictory self-descriptions across accounts.
