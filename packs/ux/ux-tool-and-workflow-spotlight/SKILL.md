# UX Tool and Workflow Spotlight

Field pack skill for **UX/Product Research and Design**. Covers a specific tool, platform, or workflow, especially AI-assisted design tools, vibe-coding platforms, and design-to-code pipelines, an area that moves fast enough and attracts enough hype-driven content that honesty and specificity matter more here than in most other content this system produces.

## Field match check (required first step)

Check whether the user's stated field(s) in `creator-context` include UX, product research, product design, UX engineering, or a clear equivalent. If not, surface a soft warning and ask whether to proceed anyway.

## Establishing firsthand experience (this determines everything else)

Ask directly: has the user actually used this tool or workflow themselves, or do they want a general overview based on what's publicly known about it? This distinction matters more here than almost anywhere else in the system.

- **Firsthand**: this is the stronger, more credible content. Ask what they actually did with it, a specific task or project, what worked well, what didn't, any friction or limitation they hit personally. Ground the piece in that specific experience rather than generic capability claims.
- **Not firsthand**: proceed more cautiously. State plainly in the content itself that this is based on available information, not hands-on use. Don't write it in a voice that implies personal testing when there wasn't any, and don't present marketing claims from the tool's own materials as if they were an independent assessment.

## Verify current information

Tools and platforms in this space change fast, features, pricing, and capabilities shift often. Search to confirm current details before writing anything specific about what a tool does or costs, don't rely on potentially outdated general knowledge about a fast-moving product. If search results are unclear or conflicting about current capabilities, say so rather than presenting stale or uncertain information as settled fact.

## Honesty requirement (this is the core discipline of this skill)

This space has a lot of uncritical hype content. Actively resist writing an uncritical puff piece:

- Name genuine tradeoffs, not just strengths, every tool optimizes for something at the cost of something else
- Be specific about **where human judgment remains load-bearing**, what the tool speeds up versus what it can't actually replace
- Avoid absolute superiority claims ("this is the best tool for X") without qualifying the specific use case or context that claim applies to
- If quoting the tool's own marketing description of itself, keep it to genuine attribution and standard copyright limits, don't let a company's own claims about itself substitute for independent assessment

## Disclosure

Ask the user directly: do they have any paid, affiliate, or partnership relationship with the tool or company being discussed? If so, that needs to be disclosed in the post itself, not omitted, this is a trust and transparency issue for the audience, and the user should decide how to disclose it, but the disclosure itself isn't optional if such a relationship exists.

## Structuring the content

Cover, adapted to whether the piece is firsthand or overview-based:
- **What it is and what it's for**, plainly, no marketing language
- **What it actually does well**, specific to a task or use case, not generic praise
- **Where it falls short or where judgment is still needed**, the honesty core of this skill
- **Where it fits relative to traditional process**, does it replace a step, augment it, or just speed up part of it

## Format

Produce a **short-form draft** (a quick, honest take) and a **long-form draft** (the fuller breakdown), per the user's platform format classifications in `creator-context`, following the confirmed house style.

## Presenting the output

Lead with whether this is firsthand experience or an overview, so the reader knows how to weight the content, followed by both drafts, clearly labeled. If a disclosure is needed, make sure it's visibly present in the drafted copy, not just noted to the user separately.
