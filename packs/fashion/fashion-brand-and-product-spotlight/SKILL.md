# Fashion Brand and Product Spotlight

Field pack skill for **Fashion**. Covers a specific garment, brand, or collection. Fashion and beauty influencer content has one of the most scrutinized disclosure histories of any content category, gifted items, affiliate links, and sponsored posts are the norm, and undisclosed versions of all three have drawn real regulatory attention. The disclosure discipline in this skill is treated as non-negotiable, not a nice-to-have.

## Field match check (required first step)

Check whether the user's stated field(s) in `creator-context` include fashion design, fashion blogging, styling, or a clear equivalent. If not, surface a soft warning and ask whether to proceed anyway.

## Establishing firsthand experience (this determines everything else)

Ask directly: has the user actually worn, tried, or handled this garment or product themselves, or do they want a general overview based on what's publicly known about it?

- **Firsthand**: this is the stronger, more credible content. Ask what they actually experienced, fit, fabric feel, construction quality, how it wore or held up. Ground the piece in that specific experience rather than generic praise.
- **Not firsthand**: proceed more cautiously. State plainly in the content itself that this is based on available information, not personal wear. Don't write it in a voice that implies personal experience when there wasn't any, and don't present a brand's own marketing description as if it were an independent assessment.

## Disclosure (non-negotiable in this skill)

Ask the user directly, and don't skip this even if they don't bring it up first: was this item gifted, provided for review, discounted or comped, or is there an affiliate link, discount code, or paid partnership involved? Fashion and beauty content has a long, well-documented history of exactly this kind of relationship going undisclosed, resist treating it as unremarkable just because it's common in the space.

If any such relationship exists, it must be disclosed in the post itself, not omitted or only mentioned to the user separately. The user decides how to phrase the disclosure, but including it isn't optional. If the user is unsure whether something counts (a long-standing brand relationship, a press loan expected to be returned), err toward recommending disclosure rather than assuming it's minor enough to skip.

## Honesty requirement, including on fit and sustainability

Resist writing an uncritical promotional piece, even without any financial relationship:

- Name genuine tradeoffs, not just strengths, quality, price, fit, versatility rarely all land in the same place for the same item
- **Be careful with fit claims specifically**: fit is highly individual, and a single person's experience ("runs small," "true to size") doesn't generalize. Qualify fit observations as the user's own experience with their body and sizing, not a universal claim about the garment
- **Be careful with sustainability or ethical production claims specifically**: don't assert a brand is "sustainable" or "ethically made" based on marketing language alone, these are genuinely contested and easy to overstate. If the user wants to make such a claim, ask what it's actually based on (a specific certification, transparency the brand has published, the user's own research), and if it's not well-grounded, suggest a more precise, defensible framing instead
- Avoid absolute superiority claims ("this is the best [item] out there") without qualifying the specific use case, budget, or body type that claim applies to

## Structuring the content

Cover, adapted to whether the piece is firsthand or overview-based:
- **What it is and what it's for**, plainly, no marketing language
- **What it actually does well**, specific, not generic praise
- **Where it falls short**, fit issues, quality concerns, price relative to comparable options, the honesty core of this skill
- **Who it's actually a good fit for**, body type, budget, use case, since "great item" is almost always context-dependent

## Format

Produce a **short-form draft** (a quick, honest take) and a **long-form draft** (the fuller review), per the user's platform format classifications in `creator-context`, following the confirmed house style.

## Presenting the output

Lead with whether this is firsthand experience or an overview, so the reader knows how to weight the content, followed by both drafts, clearly labeled. If a disclosure is needed, make sure it's visibly present in the drafted copy itself, not just noted to the user separately.
