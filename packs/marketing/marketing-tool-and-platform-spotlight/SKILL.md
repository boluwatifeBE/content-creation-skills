# Marketing Tool and Platform Spotlight

Field pack skill for **Business Marketing**. Covers a specific tool, ad platform, or martech product. This content category has an unusually high rate of undisclosed paid or affiliate promotion across the industry, which makes the disclosure discipline in this skill non-negotiable, more so than almost anywhere else in this system.

## Field match check (required first step)

Check whether the user's stated field(s) in `creator-context` include marketing, growth marketing, digital marketing, or a clear equivalent. If not, surface a soft warning and ask whether to proceed anyway.

## Establishing firsthand experience (this determines everything else)

Ask directly: has the user actually used this tool or platform themselves, or do they want a general overview based on what's publicly known about it?

- **Firsthand**: this is the stronger, more credible content. Ask what they actually did with it, a specific campaign or task, what worked, what didn't, any real limitation they hit. Ground the piece in that specific experience rather than generic capability claims.
- **Not firsthand**: proceed more cautiously. State plainly in the content itself that this is based on available information, not hands-on use. Don't write it in a voice that implies personal testing when there wasn't any, and don't present the tool's own marketing claims about itself as if they were an independent assessment.

## Verify current information

Marketing tools and ad platforms change features, pricing, and policies often. Search to confirm current details before writing anything specific, don't rely on potentially outdated general knowledge. If search results are unclear or conflicting about current capabilities or pricing, say so rather than presenting stale information as settled fact.

## Disclosure (non-negotiable in this skill)

Ask the user directly, and don't skip this even if they don't bring it up first: do they have any paid partnership, affiliate link, referral code, or free access arrangement with the tool or company being discussed? This is standard in marketing content specifically, which makes it easy to treat as unremarkable, resist that. If any such relationship exists, it must be disclosed in the post itself, not omitted or only mentioned to the user separately. The user decides how to phrase the disclosure, but including it isn't optional.

If the user is unsure whether a relationship (like a long-standing free account from the company) needs disclosure, err toward recommending disclosure rather than assuming it's minor enough to skip, audience trust in this specific content category has been eroded by exactly this kind of ambiguity being resolved in the direction of non-disclosure.

## Honesty requirement

Resist writing an uncritical promotional piece, even without any financial relationship:

- Name genuine tradeoffs, not just strengths, every tool optimizes for something at the cost of something else
- Be specific about what it's actually good for, and what it isn't, rather than broad claims of usefulness
- Avoid absolute superiority claims ("this is the best tool for X") without qualifying the specific use case or business size that claim applies to
- If quoting the tool's own marketing description of itself, keep it to genuine attribution and standard copyright limits, don't let a company's own claims substitute for independent assessment

## Structuring the content

Cover, adapted to whether the piece is firsthand or overview-based:
- **What it is and what it's for**, plainly, no marketing language
- **What it actually does well**, specific to a task or use case, not generic praise
- **Where it falls short or where a different tool might fit better**, the honesty core of this skill
- **Who it's actually a good fit for** (business size, budget, use case), since "best" is almost always context-dependent in this category

## Format

Produce a **short-form draft** (a quick, honest take) and a **long-form draft** (the fuller breakdown), per the user's platform format classifications in `creator-context`, following the confirmed house style.

## Presenting the output

Lead with whether this is firsthand experience or an overview, so the reader knows how to weight the content, followed by both drafts, clearly labeled. If a disclosure is needed, make sure it's visibly present in the drafted copy itself, not just noted to the user separately.
