# Marketing Campaign Teardown

Field pack skill for **Business Marketing**. Critiques a public campaign, ad, funnel, or email sequence using real marketing frameworks. The subject is a real company's public marketing effort, so accuracy and fairness matter as much here as in the other packs' teardown skills, and this field carries a specific extra risk: actual campaign performance (conversion rates, ROAS, revenue impact) is almost never public, which makes it especially tempting to assert results that were never actually confirmed.

## Field match check (required first step)

Check whether the user's stated field(s) in `creator-context` include marketing, growth marketing, digital marketing, or a clear equivalent. If not, surface a soft warning and ask whether to proceed anyway.

## Getting the subject

Ask whether the user has a specific campaign, ad, or funnel in mind, or wants one found.

- **If they have one**: use search to verify current, accurate details, what the campaign actually is, when it ran or launched, and whether the company or any credible outlet has published anything about its actual performance. Most campaigns have no public performance data at all, confirm this rather than assuming.
- **If they want one found**: search for a recent or currently discussed campaign relevant to the user's niche, bring back a small number of real candidates and let the user choose.

## Separating fact from read (critical distinction for this skill)

- **Confirmed facts**: what the campaign actually shows, the creative, the offer, the CTA, the targeting that's publicly visible, the channel it ran on, and anything the company or a credible source has actually published about performance
- **Interpretive read**: any claim about whether the campaign worked, what it converted at, what the strategic thinking behind it was, unless directly confirmed by a published source

**Never assert that a campaign "performed well" or "converted strongly" without a real, cited source for that claim.** This is the single most common inaccuracy in marketing commentary, treating a campaign's existence or a personal reaction to its creative quality as if it were evidence of results. If no performance data is available, which is the normal case, frame the entire critique around craft and strategic reasoning, not assumed outcomes: "this framing does a strong job of X" rather than "this converted well because of X."

## Analysis, using real frameworks

Once the subject and known facts are established, analyze using actual marketing frameworks relevant to what's observable:

- **Funnel-stage fit**: does the campaign match where its likely audience actually is in the buying journey, or is it mismatched (a hard-sell CTA aimed at a cold, unaware audience)
- **Positioning and differentiation**: does the campaign clarify what makes the offer distinct, or blend into category noise
- **Message and CTA clarity**: is the value proposition clear, is the ask specific and low-friction relative to what's being requested
- **Channel fit**: does the format and message make sense for where it's actually appearing

Choose whichever of these actually apply to the visible material, don't force all of them onto every teardown.

## Fairness and tone

Critique the strategic and creative choices, not the people or team who made them. Avoid personal attacks on named individuals. A teardown can be genuinely critical, but it should be substantive criticism grounded in the frameworks above and actually visible evidence, not a dismissive reaction dressed up as analysis. Don't fabricate quotes and attribute them to real people, and follow standard copyright limits on anything actually quoted from a source.

## Generating the content

Produce one **short-form draft** and one **long-form draft**, per the user's platform format classifications in `creator-context`, following the confirmed house style. Keep the fact/read distinction visible in the copy itself, a reader should be able to tell what's actually confirmed versus the user's own strategic read.

## Visual note

This skill cannot reproduce the campaign's actual copyrighted creative directly. Suggest the user attach their own screenshot of the actual ad or campaign material when they post, rather than generating or recreating it.

## Carousel option

A point-by-point critique often works well as a carousel, each framework point or fact-vs-read observation becoming its own slide. Ask the user if they'd rather present this critique as a carousel instead of (or alongside) the two-draft output, and if so, hand off to `social-copywriting`'s carousel structure rather than building slides here.

## Presenting the output

Lead with a one or two sentence summary of the subject and the core angle, followed by both drafts, clearly labeled. If the user wants a more one-sided piece (all-critical or all-complimentary) rather than a balanced teardown, that's their call, ask if unclear which they want.
